The Tidewhistle Cove Gazette comes out every Friday, and Agnes reads it cover to cover over breakfast. This week the back page had a notice from the stargazing club: "New moon this Saturday. The darkest night of the month. Perfect for spotting meteors. Bring a blanket and a flask." ⭐

On Saturday evening the club met on the clifftop with their telescopes and a special guest: Miss Clemo, an astronomer from the city staying at the inn. They had borrowed a beautiful antique star chart from the museum, spread out on a little table in the club's tent. At eleven everyone went outside to look at the sky. At midnight, the star chart was gone.

Three people had left the clifftop early:

Demelza Rowe left at half past ten. Captain Quill walked her home, and they were both at her cottage before eleven.
Miss Clemo left at about a quarter past eleven, saying she was tired: "I walked back to the inn along the cliff path. It was such a lovely night. The moon was full and bright, so bright I could see every pebble without a torch. I never went back into the tent."

Agnes thought of the little notice on the back page of the Gazette.
