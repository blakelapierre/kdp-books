# Clue audit: uniqueness, elimination and plain-language ambiguity check

Generated by `src/verify.py`. For every case the solver was re-run from scratch on the printed register (data.json) with the exact predicate behind each clue. Checks performed in code:

1. **Unique solution**: brute force over every suspect; exactly one satisfies all clues, and it is the named culprit.
2. **Every clue eliminates someone**: applying clues in printed order, each clue clears at least one remaining suspect.
3. **The final clue matters**: at least two suspects remain before the last clue.
4. **Necessity (extra, informational)**: how many suspects would survive if that single clue were removed (1 = clue is implied by the others, which is allowed since clue order matters; >1 = clue is strictly needed).
5. **Self-containment**: every group rule (cities, jobs, values) lists its members explicitly. Lists of cities are introduced as "the sixteen home cities that Frostwood guests come from" (all sixteen appear in the complete Case 12 register), so a listed city that happens to have no suspect in a smaller case simply clears nobody and is not a contradiction. No clue wording claims a value appears in a given register.

Relational clues (roommates, across the hall, next door, above/below) are evaluated against the whole printed register, including suspects already crossed off, exactly as the How to Solve page and each clue's wording state.

## Plain-language ambiguity review

Each clue was read on its own, as a solver would, and checked against the questions below. Every clue ends with an italic rule line that restates the condition exactly, so the story text can be colourful without being the thing the solver has to interpret.

- **Groups are always enumerated.** Any clue about a kind of city (snowy, warm, seaside, Eastern Time, subway, Canadian, two-word names) or a group of jobs lists every member in the clue itself. No outside geography is needed, and the printed list is authoritative even where real-world facts are debatable (e.g. Toronto in "snow lasts all winter"). Miami was removed from the subway list because its Metrorail is elevated; Phoenix and Los Angeles are described as places where it "practically never snows".
- **Inclusive ranges are explicit.** Letter ranges say "from A to L" / "N to Z"; floors are listed by number; "seven or more letters"; "greater than" (strict) for Table vs. floor; "fewer letters" (strict) for first name vs. surname; "comes later in the alphabet" (strict) for initials.
- **Negations and either/or logic are spelled out.** Case 7 *Plaid or Bunny* says "Exactly one is true ... (Not both, not neither.)". Case 9 *The Grey Lady* and Case 10 *Two Possibilities* say "At least one is true" (inclusive or). Case 12 *If the Movie, then Sneakers* states both branches (Movie → Sneakers; not Movie → Boots).
- **Room geometry is defined inside every clue that uses it**, not only on the How to Solve page: West Wing = last two digits 01–10; East Wing = 11–20; odd = lake side, even = mountain side; across the hall = odd room faces the next even number; next door = two numbers away on the same floor and side, with the end rooms (01, 20) called out; directly above/below = same last two digits, one floor up/down, with the edge cases stated (5th floor has only the attic above; 1st floor has only the cellar below).
- **Relational clues say who counts.** Roommate/neighbour/above/below/across clues refer to guests "listed on this register", and How to Solve states that crossed-off guests still count for these. In Case 12 every guest in the lodge is listed and the empty rooms are named in the register note, so "occupied" and "empty" are unambiguous.
- **Roommates.** "Shares a room" = another guest with the same room number. *Two Teacups* means exactly two guests in the room. *A Family Affair* defines relatives as sharing a surname.
- **Column clues use the exact printed values** (e.g. "Topping is not Nothing", "Heat is not 1", "Rehearsal is not 6 p.m."). The code check above confirms every value named in a rule line appears in that case's register.
- **No clue depends on another clue's story.** Alibi clues state the group and the time; clues that only make sense in combination (e.g. *Cinnamon Sugar* after *In the Oven*) still have a standalone rule line that is correct on its own. The solution tables' "cleared" descriptions describe who is cleared at that step when clues are applied in order.
- **Word scan.** Stories, clues, hints and solutions were scanned for violent or Christmas-specific words: none found. The only use of "hurt" is the promise that nobody gets hurt.
